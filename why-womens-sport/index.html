Our Proving Ground

Why women's elite sport.

You can’t be what you can’t see. And it’s hard to back what no one can see.

Women’s sport is growing fast, and the pathway is where its next athletes are being built. Most of the support that turns ability into recognition still arrives once an athlete reaches the top.

Lyle’s Lens brings some of that forward, building the athlete’s and the programme’s identity while they’re still developing so their value can be seen and used earlier. The idea suits any emerging athlete. Women’s sport is where it matters most right now, because support for pathway athletes hasn’t kept pace with the game’s growth.

The pathway is full of genuinely competitive sport, and that creates opportunities for a different set of brands, particularly local and regional businesses that want to be connected to the clubs and athletes in their community.7 It’s a different market from the elite game, and a real one. Sponsors get more out of it too: photos of their brand with the athletes and moments their community follows, ready to use in their own channels.

Why women’s elite sport

Elite athletes are built long before anything is built around them.

Women’s elite sport is developing its own commercial proposition. That gives organisations good reason to start building photography around athletes earlier, at pathway level.

Sporting organisations invest heavily in an athlete’s physical potential. They rarely invest in her commercial potential at the same stage.

Other systems handle this differently. The US college system puts development, identity, media and commercial support inside one institution, before an athlete turns professional. Australian pathway sport has no equivalent. That work has to happen inside the programmes themselves, or it doesn’t happen at all.

A planned photo library turns that developing potential into something the organisation and the athlete can both use. One library can serve participation, pathway recruitment and commercial activation.

For a pathway programme, the next step is to build that library around its athletes while they’re still emerging. It gives the programme evidence that it’s visible, credible and investable. It also gives the athlete an asset that works for her now and can travel with her.

The photos are made for the market. They give sponsors imagery and an athlete identity they can activate. They also give the organisation more ways to build commercial value around her throughout the pathway.

What Visibility Is Worth

One kind of visibility does three jobs.

Adolescent girls whose role models play sport are more physically active,2 and a lack of visible role models is one of the documented barriers to girls’ participation.3 The year after the AFLW arrived, 21,000 more women were playing Australian Football.4

Pathway programmes can also use that visibility to reach the next generation of athletes.

When an athlete is known beyond her performance, sponsors have something more tangible to build a relationship around.

Inspiration on its own fades with the news cycle. Lyle’s Lens builds a photo library so the organisation and the athlete can keep using those moments. They go into recruitment, funding submissions, sponsor activation, communications and the athlete’s own professional identity.

The Commercial Case

Women’s sport is developing its own sponsorship model for its own market.

Women’s elite sport is growing fast. Deloitte projects global revenue will pass US$3 billion for the first time in 2026. As leagues become independent entities, separate valuation and investment are opening up.6 Fans of women’s sport show markedly higher affinity for the brands that back it than general sports fans do.5 Sponsors are choosing women’s sport for what it offers them: a different relationship with audiences, and a closer link to the athletes those audiences follow.

That changes what a valuable sponsorship asset looks like.

A sponsor needs more than exposure. It needs an athlete to introduce, a story to tell, imagery to use and a relationship that goes beyond game day.

At pathway level, that opportunity arrives before an athlete is widely known. Organisations that build the photos around her early are better placed to give sponsors something to activate. They’re also ready as her commercial value grows.

Building a sustainable career can start in the pathway, well before an athlete’s commercial value is obvious to everyone.
