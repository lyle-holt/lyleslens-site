Case Record 01

Hockey ACT — Athlete Identity Programme

The Problem

Emerging elite athletes in Canberra are tracking toward national pathway selection and state representation, often training at a full load while also working to support themselves.1 As global women's sport revenue is projected to hit US$3 billion in 2026, up 25% in a year,2 elite programmes elsewhere in Australia — including the AFL — back their athletes' trajectory with decision-grade visual evidence that strengthens the case in board papers, funding submissions and sponsor conversations.3 Canberra programmes are already riding that growth; my framework is what capturing more of it, for the organisation and the athlete alike, looks like.

The Gap

My assessment: existing social media coverage documented what happened on the field — not the programme behind it. Missing was a standardised identity layer for a selection case, documentation built for governance and funding audiences, and a values-aligned narrative connecting on-field performance to community, culture and wellbeing — the layers this architecture was built to close.

The Architecture

A centralised, modular asset library built across six layers: standardised identity portraits for selection and credentialing; Pride Round and Reconciliation Round coverage for community and values reporting; coaching and support-staff documentation of the programme behind the athletes; complete-result coverage that preserves institutional memory of both outcomes; and Livin for Hockey coverage for athlete wellbeing and culture. One production, engineered once, deployable across every use case below.

The Result

Decision-grade imagery, delivered with full release compliance and ready to deploy the moment it arrives. And because it was architected rather than just shot, the same library is now doing work well outside its original brief — see below.
