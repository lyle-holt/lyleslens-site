Our Proving Ground

Why women's elite sport.

Women's elite sport in Australia is not an emerging market. It is an underbuilt one. The athlete quality is there. The funding pressure is there. The sponsor scrutiny is arriving. What is missing is the visual evidence.

The Participation Gap

Half of all girls leave sport before they finish school.

By the time an Australian girl turns seventeen, the odds are that she has already stopped playing. Dropout among girls aged fifteen and over runs to sixty per cent, nearly half again the rate among boys — even though two in three girls say sport is what makes them feel most confident in themselves. The reasons rarely trace back to the game itself. They trace back to what surrounds it: body image, social pressure, a competition structure built around someone else's career, and a shortage of anyone visibly further along the same path.

The Role Model Effect

Girls stay when they can see someone to become.

Visibility is not a marketing consideration here; it is a retention strategy. Six in ten young Australian women say they grew up without a female sports star to look up to, and the gap shows in the numbers that follow a career once it starts. Where visible role models exist, participation moves — AFLW alone added 21,000 players in a single year, reaching 176,000 nationally. A UK study found two-thirds of girls traced their own interest in sport directly to a female athlete they had seen; researchers in Toronto found the presence of a role model was, on its own, enough to reduce dropout.

The Commercial Case

The fans are worth more. The infrastructure is missing.

Where the audience already exists, the investment case tends to build itself. Fans of women's sport watch for longer, spend more, and show three times the brand affinity of the average sports fan — a return on attention that most partnership decks still undersell. What has not caught up is the infrastructure behind it: the asset libraries, the reporting standards, the sponsor-ready imagery that established men's programmes take for granted, and that emerging women's programmes are still building from scratch.

The Just Cause

A world where women athletes are fully visible.

Because the value they create does not end at the final whistle. It is why this remains the proving ground: not because women's elite sport needs a lesser version of the same service, but because it is where visual evidence is asked to work hardest — and where the return on making it work is greatest.
