About

34 years producing evidence for people who carried the risk.

Lyle Holt spent 34 years making judgement calls under uncertainty, where getting them wrong had real consequences.

Not creative judgement. Operational judgement.

What mattered. What was missing. What was being overlooked. What would disappear when a competing priority arrived.

He learned to recognise what others had stopped noticing.

For Lyle, that means looking beyond the obvious photograph — understanding the people, the programme, the investment and the perspective of the person who will eventually see the image.

Lyle photographs your athletes. But every frame is built to answer the questions a board, a funder or a sponsor is about to ask.

Can this programme represent us?
Can this programme justify our funding support?
Can this partnership withstand scrutiny as valuable, defensible and renewable at board level?

Operational Background

34 years in the Royal Australian Navy and Air Force

Lyle served 34 years in the Royal Australian Navy and Air Force across operational deployments, aviation, senior command and disruptive innovation.

He commanded international operations in Japan involving nine nations supporting the defence of the Korean Peninsula. He led more than 500 people operating Australia's air and space defence surveillance network. He flew the F-111 and built Number 5 Flight from scratch to establish Australia's medium-altitude, long-endurance uncrewed aircraft capability.

As Director of Plan Jericho, he challenged established assumptions and accelerated new approaches from concept to practical capability.

None of that is relevant because of the titles. It is relevant because of the environments in which he learned to work.

Lyle's LinkedIn profile → — full operational background, appointments and professional record.

His focus on women's elite sport is informed by experiences across his military career that repeatedly exposed the same problem from different angles: whether women had space to participate, whether systems actually accommodated them, and whether their contribution was visible.

Why Women's Sport sets out the case for building that infrastructure at pathway level, while an athlete is still emerging — well before her organisation would otherwise think to build it for her.

Kabul, 2009. What access to sport made possible for a girl here — and what its absence cost her — is the throughline running from Lyle's military career to his work in women's elite sport today.

Business Information

Veteran-owned and Indigenous-owned

Lyle's Lens Photography is a veteran-owned and Indigenous-owned Australian business, based on Ngunnawal Country, Canberra.

Lyle's service has been featured in Our Mob Served: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories of War and Defending Australia (Aboriginal Studies Press, 2019), the Serving Country Indigenous veterans portrait project, and a family service feature in The Guardian Australia.

His family's history of service — from his grandfather at Gallipoli, through his uncle's wartime imprisonment on the Sumatran Death Railway and later service in Korea, to his own 34 years — is documented by the United Nations Command and the Australian War Memorial.
