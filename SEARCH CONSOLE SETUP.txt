CONNECTING GOOGLE SEARCH CONSOLE TO LYLESLENS.COM
Prepared 1 September 2026. Do this AFTER pushing the files, not before.

WHY IT MATTERS

Search Console is Google's own free report on your site. It tells you which
searches people typed before they saw you, which pages they landed on, how
often you appeared, how often they clicked, and which pages Google has
refused or failed to index. Without it there is no way to know whether any
of the work done on 1 September has any effect. Everything in the outside
review was recommended without data, because no data exists yet. This is how
that stops being true.

It is free, it takes about ten minutes, and the only slightly fiddly part is
proving you own the domain.


BEFORE YOU START

You need two things:
  - a Google account (any one you use regularly; use one you will still have
    in five years, not a throwaway)
  - access to wherever lyleslens.com's DNS is managed. That is the registrar
    or host you bought the domain from and where the records pointing it at
    GitHub Pages already live. If you don't know who that is, look for the
    annual renewal invoice.


STEP 1 — CREATE THE PROPERTY

Go to https://search.google.com/search-console and sign in.

Click "Add property". You will be offered two kinds:

  DOMAIN            lyleslens.com
  URL PREFIX        https://lyleslens.com

CHOOSE DOMAIN. It covers www and non-www, http and https, and every
subdirectory, all in one property. URL prefix would cover only the exact
address you type, which on a site that has been served at both / and
/index.html is exactly the ambiguity we just spent this work removing.

Type lyleslens.com — no https://, no www, no trailing slash — and continue.


STEP 2 — PROVE YOU OWN IT

Google will show you a TXT record. It looks like:

  google-site-verification=aBcDeF1234...

Copy it. Then go to your DNS provider and add a new record:

  Type      TXT
  Name      @        (some providers want the field left blank, or want
                      "lyleslens.com" — all three mean the same thing:
                      the root of the domain)
  Value     the google-site-verification=... string, pasted whole
  TTL       leave the default

Save it, go back to Search Console, click Verify.

If it fails, wait and try again. DNS changes can take anywhere from a few
minutes to a few hours to propagate. Failing once immediately after saving
is normal and means nothing.

DO NOT DELETE THAT TXT RECORD afterwards. Google re-checks it periodically
and will drop your access if it disappears.


STEP 3 — SUBMIT THE SITEMAP

Once verified, in the left sidebar click Sitemaps.

In the "Add a new sitemap" box, type:

  sitemap.xml

and submit. The full address is https://lyleslens.com/sitemap.xml — Search
Console fills in the domain part for you.

Status will say "Couldn't fetch" for a while. That usually means Google
hasn't got to it yet, not that anything is wrong. Check back the next day.
It should end up reading "Success" with 12 discovered URLs.


STEP 4 — ASK FOR THE IMPORTANT PAGES TO BE INDEXED

Sitemaps are an invitation, not a queue ticket. For the pages that did not
exist before 1 September, you can ask directly.

Paste each of these into the search bar at the very top of Search Console
(the one labelled "Inspect any URL"), one at a time, then click "Request
indexing":

  https://lyleslens.com/sports-photography-canberra/
  https://lyleslens.com/athlete-photography/
  https://lyleslens.com/sports-media-day/
  https://lyleslens.com/evidence/hockey-act-athlete-identity/
  https://lyleslens.com/evidence/afl-canberra-media-day/

There is a daily limit of about ten requests, so do these five and leave the
rest to the sitemap.


WHAT TO LOOK AT, AND WHEN

Nothing useful will be there for a week or two. Then:

  PERFORMANCE — the only report that matters at first. It lists the actual
  search terms people used. Switch on the "Queries" tab and set the date
  range to the last three months. The review that prompted this work picked
  its target phrases without any data at all; this is the data. If nobody
  is searching "sports photographer Canberra" but plenty are searching
  something adjacent, the pages should be re-aimed at what people actually
  type.

  PAGES (under Indexing) — shows which pages Google has indexed and which it
  has skipped, with reasons. Worth one look a month. If a new page still
  says "Discovered — currently not indexed" after a month, that page is not
  earning its place and needs either better internal links or better content.

  Ignore everything else for now.


ONE THING TO WATCH FOR

Search Console may report /index.html and / as separate pages for a while,
and may flag one as a duplicate. That is expected and it is the problem the
canonical tags added on 1 September are there to fix. Google usually takes
several weeks to settle on the preferred address. Don't act on it before
then.


IF YOU ALSO WANT VISITOR NUMBERS

Search Console tells you what happened in Google. It does not tell you what
people did once they arrived. That is a separate tool (Google Analytics or a
lighter, privacy-respecting alternative) and a separate decision, involving a
tracking script on every page and a privacy notice. Not required, not urgent,
and worth deciding deliberately rather than by default.
